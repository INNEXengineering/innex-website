  - q: When is a formal root cause analysis worth the effort?
    a: When a failure has safety or environmental consequences, causes significant downtime or repair cost, or keeps recurring. Replacing a failed part without understanding why it failed often means the failure will happen again.
  - q: How does vibration analysis support failure analysis?
    a: Vibration data can reveal misalignment, imbalance, looseness, bearing defects, and resonance, often well before a failure. After a failure, historical vibration data can show how the problem developed and whether it was detectable.

## FMEA and root cause analysis consulting

Recurring equipment failures, unexplained downtime, and new designs entering service all benefit from a structured look at how things fail. FMEA and root cause analysis consulting applies established methods to identify failure modes before they occur, and to establish the true cause of failures that already have.

Failure Mode and Effects Analysis ranks potential failures by severity, likelihood, and detectability, so resources go where they matter. Root cause analysis works in the other direction: from a failure that has occurred, back through the physical mechanism, to the design, operating, and maintenance factors behind it. Used together, the two methods connect what has failed in the past to what is most likely to fail next.
