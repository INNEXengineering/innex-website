## Premises liability expert witness analysis

Falls and other injuries on commercial, warehouse, and industrial properties often involve conditions that can be measured and compared against objective requirements: stair geometry, handrail and guardrail height, changes in level, openings, and lighting. A premises liability expert witness documents those conditions, identifies the codes and standards that applied, and evaluates whether the owner or operator acted reasonably to find and correct the hazard.

The answer depends on the type of property and how it was used. A warehouse floor, a loading dock, a mezzanine, and a public walkway carry different expectations, and the inspection and housekeeping practices that would be reasonable for each differ as well.

## What we investigate

Site inspections document the conditions with measurements and photographs, which are then compared against the codes and standards in effect and the property's own inspection and maintenance records. Areas of focus include:

- Slips, trips, and falls on floors, ramps, docks, and exterior walkways
- Stairs, handrails, guardrails, and changes in elevation
- Floor and wall openings, mezzanines, and elevated platforms
- Warehouse and industrial site hazards affecting visitors, drivers, and contractors
- Lighting, signage, and visual cues
- Inspection, housekeeping, and maintenance programs
- Building and fire code compliance, as locally adopted and applicable

## Safety professional perspective on industrial premises

Paul Neumann, P.E., CMRP, CSP, is a Certified Safety Professional whose expertise includes warehouse operations and industrial plant safety. Premises cases at commercial and industrial facilities often involve the same hazards a workplace safety program is designed to control, including walking-working surfaces, fall hazards, and vehicle traffic around loading docks and equipment.

That background supports a practical evaluation of whether a property's inspection and maintenance practices were reasonable for the way the space was actually used, rather than a checklist comparison against code provisions alone. As a licensed Professional Engineer, he can also evaluate the physical design and construction of the stairs, railings, and surfaces involved.

Where industrial equipment or vehicle traffic contributed to the injury, the analysis also addresses how the property separated visitors and drivers from operating areas, and whether warnings and barriers were adequate. Each conclusion identifies the specific requirement relied on and the measurement or record that supports it.
