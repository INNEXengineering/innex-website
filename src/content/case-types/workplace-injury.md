## Workplace injury expert witness analysis

Serious workplace injuries usually trace back to a gap between how a task was supposed to be performed and how it actually was: a missing guard, energy that was not isolated, a procedure that did not fit the equipment, or training that never reached the worker. A workplace injury expert witness has to evaluate both the physical conditions and the safety management system that was supposed to control them.

That evaluation is anchored in OSHA's general industry standards and in consensus standards such as ANSI Z244.1 and NFPA 70E. It also requires judgment about what a reasonable employer in that industry would have done. The goal is a clear, documented answer to two questions: what caused the injury, and was it preventable?

## What we investigate

Each case starts with the injury mechanism and works outward to the equipment, the task, the procedures, and the people who planned and supervised the work. Typical areas of analysis include:

- Lockout/tagout programs, equipment-specific energy control procedures, and verification of isolation
- Machine guarding at points of operation, nip points, and rotating parts
- Electrical safety, arc flash hazards, and NFPA 70E work practices
- Confined space entry, hazardous materials handling, and hazard communication
- Job hazard analyses and written safety programs, and how they were carried out in the field
- Training content, frequency, and documentation for the task involved
- Contractor and multi-employer worksite responsibilities

That combination matters in workplace injury cases because the questions overlap. Whether a machine could have been guarded or locked out is an engineering question. Whether the employer's program, training, and supervision met the standard of care is a safety question. Answering both from one consistent analysis avoids the gaps that can open between separate experts.

Opinions are tied to the specific standards and documents in the record, so the basis for each conclusion is clear to counsel, to opposing experts, and to the trier of fact.
