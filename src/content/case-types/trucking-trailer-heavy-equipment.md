## Heavy equipment accident expert witness analysis

Incidents involving semi-trailers, tank trailers, yard mules, and heavy construction equipment frequently turn on mechanical condition: brakes out of adjustment, a coupling that separated, a hydraulic system that failed, or a defect that was reported and never repaired. A heavy equipment accident expert witness examines the equipment and its records to determine whether a mechanical failure occurred, and whether it should have been found and corrected.

For commercial trailers, the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations set inspection, repair, and maintenance requirements. For construction equipment, OSHA 1926 Subpart O and the manufacturer's requirements apply, along with ANSI and SAE standards where they address the specific equipment involved.

## What we investigate

Investigations typically include inspection of the trailer or equipment and any failed components; review of driver inspection reports, periodic inspection records, maintenance records, and repair invoices; and comparison with the manufacturer's maintenance requirements. Areas of focus include:

- Semi-trailer and tank trailer failures, including brakes, axles, suspensions, and coupling devices
- Yard mule and terminal tractor incidents at warehouses and distribution centers
- Heavy construction equipment failures, rollovers, and struck-by incidents
- Inspection, repair, and maintenance records and practices
- Hydraulic, structural, and wheel-end failures
- Equipment modifications and aftermarket components

## Heavy-duty truck certification and equipment expertise

Paul Neumann, P.E., CMRP, CSP, is an ASE certified technician for heavy-duty trucks, as well as passenger cars and light trucks. His areas of expertise include semi-trailers, tank trailers, yard mules, and heavy construction equipment.

That hands-on certification complements his mechanical engineering background. It allows him to evaluate both whether a component failed because of its design and whether the people responsible for inspecting and maintaining it followed the practices a competent technician would follow. Combined with his CMRP certification in maintenance and reliability, it supports clear opinions on whether a failure was foreseeable and preventable.

The analysis also considers how inspection and repair duties were divided among the equipment owner, the motor carrier, and any outside maintenance provider, since responsibility is often shared. Where a trailer was being loaded or unloaded at a warehouse or plant, his expertise in warehouse operations and materials handling equipment applies as well.
