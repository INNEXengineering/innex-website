## Forklift accident expert witness analysis

Forklifts and other powered industrial trucks are involved in a wide range of serious incidents: tip-overs, pedestrians struck in aisles and at docks, falls from loading docks, falling loads, and crushing injuries during maintenance. A forklift accident expert witness evaluates the truck, the load, the operator, and the facility together, because most incidents involve more than one of them.

OSHA 1910.178 sets specific requirements for operator training, truck examination, and safe operation, and ANSI/ITSDF B56.1 addresses the design and use of the trucks themselves. Where overhead cranes, hoists, or rigging were involved, OSHA 1910.179 and the ASME B30 standards may also apply.

## What we investigate

Investigations typically combine an inspection of the truck and the incident location with a review of training, maintenance, and facility records, and any available video or telematics data. Areas of focus include:

- Forklift tip-overs, stability, and load capacity at the load center used
- Pedestrian strikes and facility traffic management
- Falls from loading docks, trailers, and elevated platforms
- Operator training, evaluation, and certification records
- Pre-shift examinations, maintenance, and repair history
- Attachments, modifications, and data plate accuracy
- Overhead crane, hoist, and rigging incidents
- Warehouse layout, racking, and material handling practices

## Forklift train-the-trainer certification and warehouse operations expertise

Paul Neumann, P.E., CMRP, CSP, holds an &ldquo;OSHA Class 1-7 Powered Industrial Trucks: Train the Trainer Certification,&rdquo; and his areas of expertise include warehouse operations and materials handling equipment. He knows what an effective operator training and evaluation program covers, and what a facility that follows OSHA 1910.178 looks like on the floor.

That experience allows a direct comparison between the training and supervision the operator actually received and what the standard requires. Combined with mechanical engineering analysis of the truck and its maintenance history, it supports opinions on whether an incident resulted from equipment condition, operator error, inadequate training, or the way the facility managed traffic and loads.

Because powered industrial trucks are also governed by manufacturer requirements and ANSI/ITSDF B56.1, the analysis considers whether the truck itself was rated, labeled, equipped, and maintained appropriately for the work it was assigned, not only how it was driven. Where the facility layout contributed, it also addresses aisle widths, dock edge protection, and pedestrian routes.
