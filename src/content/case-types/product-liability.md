## Product liability engineering expert analysis

Product liability claims involving machinery and mechanical products typically turn on three questions. Was the product defectively designed? Was it manufactured in a defective condition? Were its warnings and instructions adequate? A product liability engineering expert answers those questions through engineering analysis of the product itself, its design history, and the circumstances of its use.

That analysis must also account for what happened to the product after it left the manufacturer, including maintenance, repairs, modifications, and how it was actually being used at the time of the incident. Consensus standards, such as ASTM standards where they apply to the product, provide a benchmark, but compliance with a standard does not by itself end the analysis.

## What we investigate

The work typically involves inspection of the product or an exemplar; review of drawings, specifications, test reports, and any FMEA or risk assessment records produced in discovery; and comparison against the standards that applied. Areas of focus include:

- Design defects and the feasibility of safer alternative designs
- Manufacturing and assembly defects, including material and weld quality
- Warnings, labels, and instructions, including ANSI Z535 format and content
- Machine guarding and risk assessment under ISO 12100 principles
- Product modifications, aftermarket parts, and foreseeable misuse
- Industrial machinery, materials handling equipment, vehicles, and agricultural equipment

## Design FMEA and mechanical design analysis

Paul Neumann, P.E., CMRP, CSP, performs Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA), Design FMEA (DFMEA), and mechanical design analysis. These are structured methods manufacturers use to identify and control hazards during product development.

Applying the same methods in litigation provides a disciplined framework for evaluating a product. It shows which failure modes a competent design process should have identified, how severe and likely each one was, and what design controls or warnings were available to address them. The result is an opinion grounded in recognized engineering practice rather than hindsight, and one that a judge or jury can follow step by step.

Where an exemplar is available, inspection and testing can compare the incident product against its design and evaluate a proposed alternative. Test protocols are designed so that results can be reproduced and examined by other experts.
